What to put on your website so customers get in touch

By the Sonnatt.ai team, Leeds · Updated 11 October 2026

The short answer

A small business website should answer the questions customers have before they contact you, and make contacting you easy. That means a clear headline saying what you do and where, your services, an idea of price, proof such as reviews and examples of your work, and an obvious way to get in touch on every page. It must also work well on a phone, load quickly, and include the legal basics such as a privacy policy.

1. Say what you do, for whom and where, straight away

Within a few seconds of landing on your homepage, a visitor should know what you do, who it's for and where you work. "Emergency plumber in Leeds" beats "Welcome to our website". Put this in your main headline, not buried further down.

2. A clear list of services

List each service in plain words. If you offer several, give each its own section or page with what it includes, who it suits and roughly how long it takes. Separate pages also help you show up when people search for that specific service.

3. An idea of price

People want to know if they can afford you before they get in touch. If you can't give exact prices, give a starting price, a typical range or explain what affects the cost. Even a rough guide filters out poor-fit enquiries and builds trust.

4. Proof that you're good at it

  • Reviews, ideally linked to your Google reviews so people can see they're real.
  • Examples of your work: photos, before-and-afters or short case studies.
  • Qualifications, memberships and insurance relevant to your trade or profession.
  • Real photos of you and your team, rather than stock images.

5. An easy way to get in touch, on every page

  • Your phone number, tappable on mobile.
  • A short contact form: name, email or phone, and what they need. Don't ask for more than you need.
  • A booking link, if customers can book online.
  • A chatbot that answers common questions and captures enquiries outside working hours.
  • A clear button such as "Get a quote" or "Book a call" near the top of each page.

6. Answers to common questions

An FAQ section answering the questions you hear every week (areas covered, payment, timescales, guarantees) saves you time and reassures customers. It also helps search engines and AI assistants understand your business.

7. Works on a phone and loads quickly

Many visitors will be on a phone. Check that text is readable without zooming, buttons are easy to tap, and pages load quickly. A slow, fiddly site loses people before they've read anything.

8. The legal and trust basics

  • A privacy policy explaining what you do with the details people give you, and a cookie notice if you use analytics or advertising cookies.
  • If you're a limited company, your website must show your registered company name, company number, the part of the UK where it's registered and your registered office address.
  • Your professional body's requirements, if you're in a regulated profession.
  • Your business address or the areas you cover, matching your Google Business Profile.

9. Keep it up to date

Out-of-date prices, old opening hours or a "latest news" post from three years ago make people wonder if you're still trading. Review your site every few months, or have someone do it for you.

Frequently asked questions

What should be on a small business homepage?

A clear headline saying what you do and where, your main services, proof such as reviews and examples of your work, and an obvious way to get in touch, such as a phone number, contact form or booking button.

Should I put prices on my website?

If you can. Exact prices, a starting price or a typical range help people decide whether to contact you and build trust. If prices vary, explain what affects the cost.

What legal information does a limited company website need?

A UK limited company's website must show its registered company name, company number, the part of the UK where it's registered and its registered office address. Most websites also need a privacy policy, and a cookie notice if they use analytics or advertising cookies.

Does my website need to work on mobile?

Yes. Many visitors will be on a phone, so text should be readable without zooming, buttons easy to tap and pages quick to load.
